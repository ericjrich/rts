01 The problem

When systems overlap, fixing the symptom is not enough.

Equipment, people, vendors, software, utilities, process, and physical infrastructure often fail at the seams between disciplines. RTS looks across those boundaries, separates symptoms from causes, and turns a messy situation into a clear path forward.

A

Ambiguous ownership

When everyone owns one piece but nobody owns the outcome, RTS helps establish the whole picture.

B

Recurring failures

Repeated resets and replacements are not root-cause analysis. The goal is to understand why the problem keeps returning.

C

Cross-discipline work

When a solution needs multiple capabilities, RTS can define the scope and coordinate qualified specialists where required.

05 The RTS method

A disciplined path from confusion to completion.

The process is designed to prevent premature solutions, unclear responsibility, and the expensive cycle of fixing the wrong thing twice.

01

Investigate

Collect facts, inspect the system, review documentation, and hear from the people closest to the problem.

02

Define

Separate symptoms from causes, identify constraints, and turn the problem into a clear scope.

03

Assemble

Determine what can be handled directly and where a specialist, vendor, or licensed trade is appropriate.

04

Coordinate

Manage sequencing, communication, decisions, documentation, and changes as the work moves.

05

Verify

Confirm the original problem was addressed, document the outcome, and identify anything still unresolved.
